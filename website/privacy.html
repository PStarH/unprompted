Unprompted privacy policy

Last updated: November 5, 2026

Unprompted is an independent open-source project developed by Xinghan Pan. This policy describes the Unprompted website and the locally run Unprompted application, including its use of Google user data.

What Unprompted does

Unprompted helps you find and organize work from sources you connect, gather supporting context, and prepare replies, documents and other actions. It runs on your device. You choose which accounts and model to connect and which external actions to authorize. The current application has no project-hosted application backend or analytics endpoint.

Google data we access and why

Connecting Google is optional. After you grant Google permissions, Unprompted accesses the following data according to the features and permissions you select:

  • Gmail: message and conversation content and metadata, including sender, recipients, subject, timestamps and message identifiers. Unprompted uses these to find requests, show their context, prepare replies and, when separately authorized, create drafts or send messages.
  • Google Drive, Docs, Sheets and Slides: file identifiers, names, metadata and supported document text or exports. Unprompted uses these to find relevant documents and provide context for your work. With separate permission and authorization, it can edit Google Docs.
  • Google Calendar: if you enable it, events and related details from your primary calendar are read to provide context for work.
  • Account and authorization information: account identifiers and OAuth credentials are used to connect the account, maintain its authorization and make the requests you enable.

Unprompted explicitly requests only the Google OAuth scopes necessary to fulfill user-directed actions, categorized as follows:

  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.readonly: Reads email message headers, sender, recipient, subject, and message content to identify incoming tasks, unaddressed requests, and related communication context.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/drive.readonly: Reads metadata and exports plain text from Google Docs, Google Sheets, and Google Slides in Google Drive to locate background reference material relevant to active tasks.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events.readonly: Reads events from your primary calendar to provide scheduling and timeline context for observed work.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.compose: Creates and manages draft email replies in Gmail, allowing you to review, edit, and confirm drafts directly in Gmail before sending.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/gmail.send: Sends email replies from your account only after you explicitly review and approve the outgoing message or enable a standing follow-up agreement for that task.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/documents: Edits or appends prepared text to Google Docs documents upon your explicit instruction and confirmation.
  • https://www.googleapis.com/auth/calendar.events: Creates or updates calendar events on your primary calendar when you schedule or confirm an appointment.

Unprompted adheres to the principle of least privilege: read-only scopes are used by default for observation, and write/send scopes (such as gmail.send or documents) are activated only when you choose to use external action features. All data accessed through these scopes is used solely to provide user-facing work-assistance capabilities on your device.

Storage, security and retention

Data in transit: All network communication between Unprompted and Google APIs, as well as connections to user-configured model providers, is strictly encrypted in transit using industry-standard TLS 1.2 or TLS 1.3 (HTTPS).

Local storage and network isolation: Unprompted is a local-first desktop application with no project-operated cloud servers, centralized database, or remote telemetry endpoints. The local server binds exclusively to the loopback interface (127.0.0.1) and is not exposed to external networks or the public internet.

Data protection at rest: OAuth tokens (including refresh tokens and access tokens), configuration settings, and cached task context are stored locally on your device in your user directory. To safeguard this data at rest, Unprompted relies on operating-system access control mechanisms (POSIX permissions 0700 for directories and 0600 for sensitive files), restricting access exclusively to your authenticated operating-system user account. When device-level full-disk encryption (such as macOS FileVault or Windows BitLocker) is enabled, all stored tokens, credentials, and cached records are encrypted at rest by the operating system. In addition, credential management integrates platform-native secure credential stores (such as the system Keychain) where available, preventing unauthorized cross-user access.

Retention: Cached task context, message identifiers, and derived records are retained locally only for as long as you maintain the connected source and require the data for active work assistance. There is no remote retention on project infrastructure.

Sharing and model processing

Unprompted contacts Google to read or perform authorized actions. When you configure a model, selected task context, which may include Google messages, documents or derived data, can be sent to that model endpoint to perform the task. If the endpoint is remote, its provider receives that data; a compatible local model can avoid that remote transfer. Authorized actions can also send data to the destination you choose, such as a message recipient or connected service. Providers have their own retention and processing terms; check them before connecting a model or service.

The Unprompted project does not sell Google user data or use it for advertising. The project does not use Google user data to train generalized AI models. Before selecting a remote model, check that its data handling, including retention and training, is compatible with the permissions and policies that apply to your Google data. The project cannot control a separately configured provider's practices.

Google data is subject to the Google API Services User Data Policy and, where applicable, the Google Workspace API User Data and Developer Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. These policies restrict uses and transfers of Google user data.

Disconnect, revoke and delete

You have full control over your data and can delete cached Google records or revoke access at any time using the following mechanisms:

  • Disconnect and clear data: In the application, navigate to Connections → Google and click Disconnect and clear data. This immediately halts all background synchronization, deletes stored Google OAuth tokens (access tokens and refresh tokens) from local configuration, and purges all cached messages, drive text extracts, calendar records, and derived tasks from the local SQLite database.
  • Granular task deletion (Forget): You can remove individual tasks or memories at any time by clicking Forget in the workspace. This permanently deletes the selected task record, its associated context, and any prepared drafts from local storage.
  • Complete local application deletion: You can permanently wipe all local application data, configuration, tokens, and logs by stopping the application and deleting the local application directory (see the storage documentation for exact paths on macOS, Linux, and Windows).
  • Revoking Google OAuth access: You can revoke Unprompted's permission at any time directly through your Google Account connections. Revocation immediately invalidates all issued OAuth tokens, preventing the application from making any further API calls to Google.

Retention period and deletion scope: Because Unprompted does not operate a cloud backend, clearing local data immediately and permanently removes all Google user data from the application with zero server-side retention. However, clearing local data or revoking access does not delete:

  • Your original emails, documents, or calendar events hosted in your Google Workspace account (Unprompted does not delete your cloud originals).
  • Data that you previously and explicitly instructed the application to transmit to an external recipient (e.g., a sent email) or to a third-party model provider you configured; such data is governed by the respective provider's terms and deletion procedures.

Other connected services

Reading another cloud source contacts that provider, and the local workspace may contain its content and related task records. External actions depend on your selected permissions and authorizations. Connected services, your organization and model providers may have their own privacy and security requirements.

This website

GitHub Pages hosts this website and receives connection information, including IP addresses, to deliver and secure it. See GitHub's Privacy Statement for its processing and retention. The site has no project-operated analytics, advertising trackers, account registration or personal-data collection forms. Its assets are served with the site; the install-command button writes to your clipboard when pressed. Language and animation preferences are kept in memory for the current page.

Questions and privacy requests

For privacy questions or requests, email xinghan090611@gmail.com. You may also use the issue tracker to request a private channel. Issues and pull requests are public; do not post credentials, workspace files or private content. For vulnerabilities, follow the security reporting instructions.

Depending on your location and who controls processing, you may have rights to access, correct, delete or obtain a copy of personal data, object to or restrict processing, or complain to a data protection authority. Contact GitHub, your organization or a connected provider about data they control; the maintainer cannot directly erase their copies.
